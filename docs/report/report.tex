\begin{abstract}
We predict the probability that an AI subject answers an unseen benchmark item correctly using only the four text fields the platform provides. Our submission embeds each item with three large embedding models, reconstructs all label-dependent features at test time from shipped training statistics, and combines per-family predictors through a three-layer non-negative linear logit stack. A per-round calibrator refits benchmark-level intercepts on the 25 revealed labels, and a temperature fitted by leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO) validation corrects a systematic underconfidence of the stack. The full stack reaches 0.4252 validation BCE against 0.4445 for the best single member. Our ablations show that ensemble depth, not member count, carries the gain, and that members which help under item-level validation can fail outright under benchmark-level shift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The task is item cold-start prediction. Every test subject appears in the training matrix but no test item does, so the only usable signal is item content, subject identity, and the benchmark and condition labels. We treat this as soft binary cross-entropy regression from item text embeddings, and our design question is how to combine many partially redundant content-based predictors so the combination survives distribution shift to unseen benchmarks.

Our answer is deliberately simple. We build three independent prediction families, one per embedding model, and merge them with a three-layer \emph{linear} logit stack fitted under non-negativity, followed by a calibration layer that adapts each scoring round to its revealed labels. We chose linear combiners over a gradient-boosted meta-learner that scores better on item-level validation (0.4218 vs.\ 0.4252) because every benchmark-level holdout we ran favored fewer parameters, and the hidden test is benchmark-shifted by construction.

Contributions, in the order a skimming reader should take away. (1) A three-layer non-negative linear ensemble over three embedding families that improves validation BCE from 0.4445 (best single member) to 0.4252, with each layer's gain measured separately (Table~\ref{tab:layers}). (2) A two-part calibration scheme, per-round benchmark intercepts fitted on the 25 revealed labels plus a LOBO-fitted global temperature $\tau=1.30$ that recovers $+0.007$ BCE on held-out benchmarks. (3) Negative results we believe generalize. An IRT member that helps under item-level validation ($+0.0011$) degrades sharply on held-out benchmarks and was dropped; index-recall is a poor proxy for the downstream cost of compressing the nearest-neighbor index; and bitwise train/serve consistency of embeddings is a real failure surface, not a formality.

\section{Training data}

We train on the public \texttt{measurement-db} corpus prepared into 4.50M (subject, item-variant) response rows covering 311{,}130 item variants, 906 subjects, and 17 benchmarks. An item variant is an (item, normalized condition) pair, matching the platform's hidden snapshot. Labels are pass \emph{rates} in $[0,1]$, not binary outcomes (29\% of rows are exactly 0 and 62\% exactly 1), so all members are trained as cross-entropy regressors on soft targets and evaluated by mean soft BCE. All validation uses grouped 3-fold cross-validation with items as groups, so every reported out-of-fold (OOF) number is item cold-start by construction. Subjects and items are keyed by content hashes, which lets the runtime recover subject identity from the raw \texttt{subject\_content} string.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Families and runtime features.}
Each family embeds the templated item text (benchmark, condition, item content) with one model: Qwen3-Embedding-8B, llama-embed-nemotron-8b (with the bidirectional-attention protocol), and LGAI-Embedding-Preview. At test time a cold item arrives as raw text, so every feature must be recomputable from its embedding plus shipped training statistics. We ship, per family, k-means centroids (256 fine, 32 coarse) for cluster geometry features, a product-quantized (PQ-512) index over the 311k training-item embeddings for nearest-neighbor features, and a sparse subject-by-item pass-rate matrix for label-derived aggregates (neighbor pass-rate statistics at $k\in\{4,8,32,64\}$, cluster difficulty, subject-cluster gaps). The full dense block is 543 features, reconstructed identically (verified to $\le 10^{-4}$ max error against training shards) by the shipped numpy-only runtime.

\paragraph{Members.}
Each family trains two member types. First, a bank of gated-GLU MLPs in a leave-one-feature-group-out (LOO) design, one variant per withheld feature group, which decorrelates the bank and lets the stack prune groups that do not earn weight. Second, a GPU random forest (depth 12, 128 trees) over the dense block plus a PCA of the embedding, ported to exact numpy inference (max deviation $9.6\times 10^{-8}$ from the training-time model). Members the selection stage rejected (XGBoost, factorization machines, kNN, denoising autoencoders, 1-D CNNs, logistic regression, IRT) are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{The three-layer linear stack.}
All combination is $z=\sum_j w_j\,\mathrm{logit}(p_j)+b$ with $w_j\ge 0$, fitted by L-BFGS-B on soft BCE. Layer~1 blends each family's LOO-MLP bank and prunes zero-weight members (8, 10, and 9 of 10 kept per family). Layer~2 blends the pruned MLP with the forest within each family. Layer~3 blends the three family probabilities (weights 0.64 nemotron, 0.25 lgai, 0.15 qwen). Each layer is fitted on honest inner-CV predictions of the layer below, so no layer fits on predictions that saw its rows.

\paragraph{Calibration and adaptive labeling.}
At test time two corrections sit on top of the stack. A global temperature $\tau$ sharpens the final logit, fitted offline by LOBO validation (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Then a per-round calibrator refits intercepts on the round's revealed labels, with partial pooling. It fits a global intercept, an extra shift for benchmarks unseen in training, and per-benchmark intercepts shrunk toward their group value (ridge strengths 20/10/20), all by Newton steps on BCE. Our acquisition function requests labels with a dual-pool stratification that targets roughly 80\% unseen-benchmark labels, because an extreme-value top-$K$ selection cannot be tuned to an interior mix by score scaling alone.

\paragraph{Ensemble depth carries the gain.}
Table~\ref{tab:layers} reports BCE at each layer. Within a family, blending the LOO-MLP bank beats the best single MLP by 0.005 to 0.015, and adding the forest gains another 0.002 to 0.003. Crossing families gains 0.012 over the best family (nemotron, 0.4274) and 0.0016 over an unweighted logit mean. Cross-family OOF correlations sit near 0.93--0.95, so the three families converge on similar models and the residual decorrelation comes from the embeddings themselves.

\paragraph{Member selection, and a warm/cold reversal.}
Under non-negative fitting on the full member pool, only the MLP bank and the forest receive weight; XGBoost, logistic regression, factorization machines, autoencoder and CNN members all go to zero as collinear with the survivors. Two members were interesting failures. A subject-embedding IRT member improved the stack by $+0.0011$ under item-level validation, but on leave-one-benchmark-out probes its predictions degraded far below the base rate on held-out benchmarks, so we excluded it. A kNN member added only $+0.0002$, its neighbor signal already captured by the forest's neighbor features. The lesson we draw is that item-level validation systematically overrates members whose signal routes through within-benchmark structure.

\paragraph{Compressing the neighbor index, and a misleading proxy.}
Shipping the three 311k$\times$4096 indices uncompressed costs 3.8\,GB. We compared int8 (recall@64 of 0.991, 1.28\,GB per family), PQ-512 (0.825, 164\,MB), and PCA, which is disqualified outright (even 2048 dimensions keeping 98.6\% of variance reaches recall 0.589; neighbor structure lives in low-variance directions). Recall suggested PQ-512 would be highly damaging. Downstream it is not. Replacing one family's 17 index-dependent feature columns with PQ-derived values and re-scoring the fixed models moves the forest by $+0.0024$ and the final stack by only $+0.0009$ BCE, because the stack's weight concentrates on features the index does not touch. We shipped PQ-512.

\paragraph{LOBO temperature.}
Fitting a single temperature per held-out benchmark and applying the median of the \emph{other} benchmarks' temperatures yields $\tau=1.30$ with a held-out gain of $+0.0066$ BCE averaged per benchmark ($+0.0074$ row-weighted, 17 benchmarks, 4.50M rows). The direction surprised us. Each non-negative linear layer fits weights summing below one, which compounds into systematic underconfidence, so the cold-start correction is to sharpen, not shrink. Because member predictions on a held-out benchmark still come from models that saw that benchmark's other items, this $\tau$ is a lower bound on the true cold sharpening; we ship it as fitted.

\section{Failure modes and patterns}

\paragraph{Item-level validation is optimistic under benchmark shift.}
Our validation BCE (0.4252) sits far below what benchmark-shifted leaderboard scores imply, while the predict-the-mean baseline scores 0.63. Probes that hold out entire benchmarks show member quality collapsing toward (and for IRT, below) the base rate. This single observation drove most of our design: linear combiners over the better-scoring tree meta-learner, dropping members that only help warm, per-round intercept refits, and the LOBO temperature. We also tried detecting out-of-distribution items by embedding distance to the training set and found it uninformative.

\paragraph{Train/serve embedding consistency is a real failure surface.}
Our features are only valid if the serve-time encoder reproduces the cached training embeddings. A minor-version library upgrade between training and serving changed the forward pass of one encoder family (identical weights and token ids, cosine to cache 0.49) and silently flipped a second family to bidirectional attention through a global attention-mask patch intended for one model. We caught both only by re-embedding training items at serve settings and comparing to the cache (the two healthy families reproduce at cosine $>0.9999$). The fix was to scope the mask patch to its model and to re-embed the broken family's full cache under the serving stack, restoring consistency by construction. We recommend cache-reproduction checks as a standard preflight for any submission that ships embedding-derived features.

\section{Limitations}

All ablation numbers are item-grouped validation and overstate benchmark-cold performance; LOBO probes reuse members trained with within-benchmark siblings, so cold estimates are floors, not unbiased estimates. The temperature and calibrator address level and slope but not feature-level transfer. The acquisition design was tuned in simulation against an assumed category structure rather than measured platform behavior.
